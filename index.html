The Story

How It All Began

I am Dom Pound, the creator of 'Doms Model Legends'. As a small boy my dad made me dozens of models — they hung from the ceiling, adorned clear glass shelves or were scattered in large 'Air Forces' on the ground. Mini worlds came to life at weekends as combat ensued in my mind.


The older I got, my dad made me more display models. I'll never forget coming downstairs as a 7 year old and finding my main Christmas present was a built 1/24 Hurricane with engine on show! I was from that moment fascinated by the detail. Even as my 1/24 collection was added to, it was always the pride and joy of the collection.


When I started building for myself it was perhaps no surprise that it was one of these beasts I tackled first. The older I got, the more detail I wanted to add. As my interest increased to armour and ships this craving for detail continued to grow. I began making my own parts, adding wiring, pipework, spending small fortunes on detail sets and making models with very bespoke and in some cases unique features.


Doms Model Legends was created in 2022 as a small side project alongside my full time work. Back then I never would have considered that the company would grow to be a registered company with a website and three large museum projects. Originally I set out to simply create a Facebook page to display my own model builds and for others to share theirs — simply to be part of the model community. However on the back of one of my infamous fully detailed Panther tank builds, private work was requested. And the rest they say is history!


Across the website is information on the three unique museum projects where models are being built as model memorials. This is all about dedication — something that is very close to my heart. With a very rich military background, I see remembrance as one of the most important things today's society can focus on. These models are being built with as much realism as is possible and then unveiled as mini memorials to the pilot/crew with their plaque of remembrance.


Model making is an art, a therapy and it is what people make it. This webpage is to showcase our work, but it's also to inspire and to encourage others who enjoy model building. Making models is about making the next one better than the last. Our story is still but a small one, but it shows what can happen if you follow your heart.

The Story

A Project Born from the Heart

The Battle of Britain has always, and always will be the subject closest to my heart. Being lucky enough to have spent many days, from a very early age, in the company of veterans at reunions and gatherings — and in my teen years at the commemorations at Cape Le Ferne, the national Battle of Britain memorial to the 'Few'.


Alas, the 'Few' and their Luftwaffe opponents are now all gone, so it is left to us to carry on remembering and making sure this, like all other conflicts, is not forgotten.


The 1940 Model project was an idea I came up with in spring 2024 — models that were to look as realistic as possible, to try and bring the reality of the wear and tear on those aircraft and the skill needed to fly them, fight in them and to maintain them.

How It Grew

From 19 Models to 880+

This was only supposed to be 19 models, across 7 museums for the recent 85th anniversary year of the Battle. But such was the interest from other museums and collections that very quickly the project outgrew this plan.


Now in 2026, this has grown into a project spanning 58 museums or collections with over 880 aircrew to be dedicated, commemorating aircraft and aircrew between March–December 1940, taking in the Battles of Norway & France and the Battle of Britain. Being almost entirely self-funded, it's an honour to be creating these mini tributes. It is projected it will take around 25+ years to complete. The sub-components are now built by Pat, allowing Dom to focus on the detailing and airbrushing.

The Story

The Most Personal Project

Following on from the 1940 project, I had always intended to create a second project dedicated to Bomber Command. Whilst the Battle of Britain is the closest subject to me, the bombing campaign is the most personal.


My Great cousin Sgt Maurice Goult was lost flying with 196 Sqdn in November 1944 and remains missing. Over 55,000 aircrew were lost with Bomber Command during WW2, and it took nearly 70 years for these men to be given an official memorial.


The project now spans nearly 30 museums and collections. Each completed model is dedicated first and foremost to the crew, but also to 1,000 men of Bomber Command. The first unveilings are July 11th at Manston and September 27th at RAF Wickenby Museum.

The Story

The Final Project (For Now)

The final project (for now) is the USAAF trail, dedicated to the men from the USAAF who were based in Britain during WW2. This covers a range of Commands including the 8th & 9th Air Forces, transport squadrons who did such sterling work during D-Day and beyond.


The project received the quickest amount of interest from both museums & collections, but more importantly from veterans' families — so much so that the project has grown to include a couple of aircraft from the 15th Air Force at selected family request.


So far 28 museums, and around 65 models. The first will go on display in March 2026.

About the Collection

A Lifelong Passion for Remembrance

As an avid Battle of Britain enthusiast it was only a matter of time before I started collecting. First I started what has grown into a massive signature collection — over 600 RAF & Luftwaffe personnel from the Battle — and now we have our own private collection of aircraft parts.


Currently the collection is mainly housed in our workshop, but from 2026 some of these pieces will be making their way out on loan to museums and in time will start to be accompanied by models in line with the 1940 project. This will allow us to coordinate with museums to tell more stories of these men and help ensure their sacrifice is not forgotten.


In time the collection will be available to view by appointment and on dedicated open days or events in the Devon & Dorset area. Dates TBC.

About BOBHS

Dom's Role as Area Manager

As an area manager for the Battle of Britain Historical Society it's my privilege to link my model work with what we do as a society. The Historical Society's mission is to make sure we preserve the memory of the Battle of Britain aircrew — which also includes making sure their headstones and memorials are maintained.


The areas I officially cover are Surrey, where the largest Commonwealth War Graves cemetery is located at Brookwood, as well as the smaller area county of Dorset. But my travels across the country to unveil the model memorials allows me to reach far and wide — the headstones around the country — making sure they are being looked after or repaired, as well as ensuring plaques are placed so that members of the public are aware that these are special graves.


We are also involved in some special projects such as fundraising for museums so long as they are relevant to our mission, as well as holding talks and gatherings to spread the stories and promote the society and recruit members.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Everything you need to know about commissioning a build with Dom.

How do I book a commission? +
Please fill out the contact form on the website, email direct at legendsmodelling1940@outlook.com, or contact us via Facebook. Dom will get back to you to discuss your requirements.
How do I pay for a commission? +
Dom believes in working with the customer to spread the cost. All builds are broken down into 'stages' and upon reaching agreed stages of the build, payment will be requested for that stage. This means you pay as the build progresses rather than all upfront.
Is there a deposit to book a slot? +
Yes. All builds are booked in advance with a £10–15 deposit to hold the build slot. This deposit comes off the final amount owed.
Do I sign anything to confirm costings? +
Yes. Upon receipt of the deposit, a disclaimer will be sent out to read and sign. This will outline cost per stage along with the final cost, build timescale, holiday dates, and a breakdown of what will be done as part of the build.
Who pays for delivery and insurance? +
Delivery of the model can be done by post — a box will need to be purchased and the cost of 1st class signed and tracked delivery is paid by the customer. If you live in the UK, hand delivery can be arranged so long as this fits within Dom's diary. Some contribution to travel may be required depending on distance.
Can I book more than one commission at once? +
Yes! If customers request it, builds can be done either at the same time or back to back, depending on diary availability.
Do I need to purchase the model kit? +
Yes — or the money can be sent and Dom will order the model to be delivered directly, which saves double delivery. This also includes any upgrade parts or decals you'd like included in the build.
Can I arrange to view the private collection? +
Yes you can! Although small at the moment we have parts from over 50 aircraft ranging from largely Battle of Britain — including parts from four Ju87 Stukas. We also have parts from Bomber Command and two D-Day C47s, including fragments from one of three lost from RAF Exeter.

It's £5 pre-paid for a visit and will include a complimentary tea or coffee. You'll also get to see where the models are created as well as viewing the collection. There are plenty of stories for such a small space. The money goes towards the upkeep and improvements to protecting these valuable objects.
How do I pay and when can I visit? +
Contact Dom and payment will be via bank transfer or PayPal. Visit days: Selected Saturdays or Sundays, or Wednesdays 4–6pm.
